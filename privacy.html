The short version. Arrows In! has no accounts and no servers of our own. Your progress, settings and purchases are stored on your device and, on Android when you are signed in to Google Play Games, also in your own Google account so they survive a reinstall or a new phone. The game uses Google services for advertising, pseudonymous usage statistics (no names, no contact details) and crash reports, and the app stores handle payments, which RevenueCat verifies for us. We never ask for your name, email, contacts, photos or location, and we never sell personal data.

1. Who is responsible for your data

Arrows In! (the “Game”) is developed and published by Dariia Tieriekhova, an independent developer based in Ukraine (“we”, “us”). Depending on which law applies to you, we are the data controller, business, personal information handler or responsible party for the processing described here.

Contact for any privacy question or request: tieriekhova.dariia@gmail.com. A postal address is available on request. Please write in English or Ukrainian.

This policy covers the Arrows In! mobile app for iOS and Android (bundle and package identifier com.arrowsin.game). It does not cover the app stores themselves, or any website you reach by leaving the Game.

2. One standard, everywhere

Privacy laws differ by country, and a game sold worldwide could try to give each player only the minimum its local law demands. We do the opposite: the protections in this policy apply to every player, in every country, whichever law you happen to live under. Where a specific law gives you more than this policy does, that law wins; section 11 lists what it adds in the largest regions. Where your country is not mentioned at all, the rest of this policy still applies to you in full, and the same contact address works.

In practice this is easier for us than for most apps, because the Game holds almost nothing about you: there is no account to breach, no password to leak and no profile to sell.

3. Your save record

The Game keeps one save record in the app’s private storage on your device. It contains:

  • which levels you have completed, your best number of moves, and the crowns you have earned;
  • any unfinished level, so you can leave the Game and come back to it;
  • your settings: sound, vibration, and light or dark appearance;
  • what you own and how much of it: the “No ads” purchase, your crown balance, Extreme levels unlocked with crowns, collection and reward-ladder rewards already received, your stock of hints and tries, and a list of the purchase transactions already delivered, so that no purchase is credited twice;
  • how far you have got in the tutorial and which one-time tips you have already seen;
  • for each level you have not yet completed, how many times you have started it and how long you have played it in total, which feed the statistics in section 4.2;
  • when you last opened the Game and when you came back after a week or more away, which can switch on a returning-player discount (section 11.1);
  • when an ad was last shown and how many levels you have completed since, so ads are spaced out, and when the Game last asked you to rate it.

The record on your device is deleted when you uninstall the Game or, on Android, clear the Game’s storage in your device settings.

Cloud copy on Android. If you are signed in to Google Play Games on your Android device, the Game also keeps a copy of the same record in your own Google account, using Google Play Games’ Saved Games feature (section 4.7). The copy is updated while you play, so your progress, crowns, hints, tries and purchases come back after a reinstall or on a new phone signed in to the same Google account. We have no server and cannot read the copy: only the Game, running on a device signed in to your account, can open it. If you are not signed in to Google Play Games, and on iOS, there is no cloud copy and the record stays only on your device.

On Android, the system’s own backup feature may also include app data in your Google account backup; that copy is governed by Google’s privacy policy and controlled by your device settings, not by us.

4. Data collected by the services we use

We have no servers. Everything the Game sends out goes to one of the providers below, each acting under its own privacy policy. None of it contains your name or contact details. Most of what we see is aggregated reports; the one exception is the pseudonymous statistics records described in section 4.2.

4.1 Advertising — Google AdMob

The Game shows three kinds of ads. A small banner at the bottom of the screen. Rewarded video ads, which you choose to watch in exchange for a hint or a try. And interstitial ads between levels: once you are past the first levels of the campaign, after some of the levels you complete, when you tap Next on the victory screen. How often interstitials appear is set remotely (section 4.4) and may change; they are never shown in the tutorial or in the Extreme section.

To serve and measure those ads, Google AdMob may process: your device’s advertising identifier (IDFA on iOS, Advertising ID on Android), IP address, from which an approximate location such as country or city is derived, device and operating system details, language and region, and interaction data about the ads shown, such as impressions, clicks and whether a video was watched to the end. AdMob also stores how recently an ad was shown so we can limit their frequency.

Whether ads are personalised depends on your choice:

  • Where consent is required by law — the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, and other countries whose rules Google’s consent framework covers — the Game asks on first launch, through Google’s consent dialog, before any advertising or statistics begin. If you decline, ads are limited to non-personalised ads, which still need basic data such as IP address and device information in order to be delivered and to prevent fraud.
  • On iOS, personalised advertising additionally requires your permission in the system’s App Tracking Transparency prompt. If you decline, the advertising identifier is not used for tracking, whatever else you have agreed to.
  • Everywhere else, you can turn off ad personalisation at any time in your device settings, and where local law gives you an opt-out from the sharing of your data for advertising, exercising it instructs Google to restrict how it processes data from your device.

See Google’s Privacy Policy and how Google uses data from AdMob apps.

4.2 Usage statistics — Google Firebase Analytics

We use Firebase Analytics to understand how the Game is played, so we can fix what is too hard, too slow or too confusing. It works with a randomly generated app instance identifier, not with your identity. The events we record are, typically: the tutorial steps being reached and finished; a level being opened, completed, restarted or abandoned, together with a summary of that attempt such as the number of moves, the time taken, how many times you started it, how many hints and tries were used and whether a crown was earned; hints, tries and crowns being used or received; ads being offered, shown, skipped, blocked or failing to load, and the estimated revenue Google reports for an ad shown; screens of the collection, the rewards, the Extreme section and the shop being opened; offers being shown; purchases and restores succeeding, being cancelled or failing; the request to rate the Game being shown; the cloud save signing in or failing; and settings being changed. Alongside events we store coarse properties of the player: which stage of the campaign you have reached and how many crowns, hints and tries you hold, each as a range rather than an exact number; the country of your app store account; whether you own “No ads”; whether you have never purchased, purchased once or more than once; the price level of the shop packs you are shown; whether this is your first session and whether you have finished the tutorial; your sound, vibration and appearance settings; whether the cloud save is on; your consent status; and which version of the Game and of its levels you are running.

Firebase can export these event records, each still keyed by the random app instance identifier, to Google BigQuery, where Google deletes them after 60 days. We keep a copy of those exported records, on our own computer and not in any shared place, so we can study how levels play over a longer period. The copy holds the same pseudonymous fields as the export and nothing more, and it is subject to the same retention limit as the rest of the statistics (section 8).

We do not send your name, email address, phone number, contacts, precise location, or the content of anything you type — the Game has no text input at all.

4.3 Crash reports — Google Firebase Crashlytics

If the Game crashes or hits a handled error, for example a save file it cannot read, Crashlytics receives a technical report: the error and its stack trace, the device model, operating system version, app version, available memory and storage, and a random installation identifier. We use it only to fix defects.

4.4 Configuration and experiments — Firebase Remote Config and A/B Testing

Some numbers in the Game — the contents and prices of shop packs, the reward ladder, the prices in crowns, how often ads appear — can be adjusted without releasing a new version, and sometimes we compare two variants to see which works better. This uses the same app instance identifier to keep your assignment stable, and adds no new category of data.

4.5 Payments — Apple App Store and Google Play

All purchases are processed by Apple or Google, depending on where you installed the Game. We never see or handle your payment details. From the store we receive confirmation that a purchase succeeded, the product identifier, and the list of your past non-consumable purchases of this Game, so that the “Restore purchases” button in the shop and our returning-player discounts can work. Your purchase history in this Game, together with how recently you have played, also determines which price level and offers you are shown; section 11.1 explains what that means and what it does not.

To check that each purchase is genuine and to restore purchases after a reinstall, the Game uses RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc.), which acts as our processor. RevenueCat receives an identifier for you: a random one it creates for this installation of the Game or, on Android when you are signed in to Google Play Games, your Google Play Games player ID, so that your purchase history follows you to a new installation. It also receives the store’s purchase token and transaction details (product, price, currency, store country, dates), your device type, operating system and app version, and the request’s IP address. It never receives your payment details, name or email. See the RevenueCat Privacy Policy.

See Apple’s Privacy Policy and Google’s Privacy Policy.

4.6 Over-the-air updates — Expo

The Game may check for small over-the-air updates to its non-native code. When it does, it asks the update service, operated by Expo, whether a newer bundle exists, sending the platform, the app version and the runtime version; the service sees the request’s IP address. No game data is sent. See the Expo Privacy Policy.

4.7 Cloud save — Google Play Games (Android)

On Android, if you are signed in to Google Play Games, the Game uses Google Play Games Services to keep your save record (section 3) in your Google account. Google signs you in automatically through the Google Play Games app, or when you tap Sign in in the Game’s settings; the Game itself never sees your Google password, name or email address. The Game receives only your Google Play Games player ID, which it uses to find your saved copy and to link your purchases in RevenueCat (section 4.5). Google stores the copy in a hidden app folder in your Google Drive that only the Game can access; it is not visible among your Drive files and is managed under Google’s Privacy Policy and your settings in the Google Play Games app.

  • We do not sell personal data for money, and we do not use it to build profiles outside the advertising described above.

7. Your choices

  • In the Game: open Settings, the gear icon on the main screen, and choose Privacy Settings to open the consent dialog again and change or withdraw your consent. This entry appears wherever the dialog applies to you.
  • iOS: Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking turns app tracking on or off for the Game.
  • Android: Settings → Privacy → Ads lets you delete or reset your advertising ID and opt out of ad personalisation.
  • Across your Google account: adssettings.google.com.
  • Stop banner and interstitial ads: the one-time “No ads” purchase removes the banner and all interstitial ads. Rewarded videos stay available if you choose to watch one for a hint or a try. Usage statistics and crash reports are unaffected by that purchase and remain governed by your consent.
  • Erase what is on your device: uninstall the Game, or on Android clear its storage in Settings → Apps → Arrows In! → Storage. How to ask for the rest to be deleted is explained on our data deletion page.
  • Cloud copy (Android): to stop it, sign out of Google Play Games or turn off automatic sign-in in the Google Play Games app; the Game then keeps your progress only on your device. To delete the copy, open Google Drive settings → Manage apps, find Arrows In! and choose Delete hidden app data, or delete the Game’s data in your Google Play Games profile settings. Uninstalling the Game does not delete the cloud copy; that is the point of it.
  • Anything else: write to tieriekhova.dariia@gmail.com. We do not charge for requests and we do not make you create an account to make one.

8. How long data is kept

  • On your device: until you uninstall the Game or clear its storage.
  • Cloud copy (Android): in your Google account until you delete it (section 7) or delete your Google Play Games profile.
  • Analytics events: retained by Google for the period we configure in Firebase, currently up to 14 months, after which they are deleted or kept only in aggregate form. The BigQuery export is deleted by Google after 60 days; our own copy of it (section 4.2) is kept for no more than 14 months from the date of each event and then deleted.
  • Crash reports: retained by Crashlytics for up to 90 days.
  • Advertising data: retained by Google according to its own policies and your ad settings.
  • Purchase records: kept by Apple or Google for as long as their terms and tax law require. RevenueCat keeps a copy linked to the random installation identifier or your Google Play Games player ID, on our behalf, for as long as it is needed to verify and restore purchases and for our accounting; we delete it on request where the law allows.
  • Support emails: up to two years, then deleted.

9. Where data goes

Google (including Google Play Games and Google Drive for the cloud copy), Apple, RevenueCat and Expo process data on servers in the United States and other countries, so data about your use of the Game will usually leave your own country. Where data is transferred out of the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, those providers rely on the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses and, where applicable, on the EU-US, UK-US and Swiss-US Data Privacy Frameworks. For transfers governed by other laws — Brazil, Canada, Australia, Japan, Korea, South Africa and others — those providers apply the safeguards their own policies describe. The only personal data we hold ourselves is the pseudonymous statistics copy in section 4.2 and support emails, both kept by us in Ukraine; we do not pass them on to anyone.

10. Rights you have wherever you live

Whatever your country, and without needing to quote a law at us, you may:

  • ask what data about your use of the Game exists and receive a copy of it;
  • ask for it to be corrected or deleted;
  • withdraw a consent you previously gave, and object to processing based on our legitimate interests;
  • opt out of personalised advertising, and of the sharing of your data for advertising;
  • ask a human to look at your request, and complain to us and to your local privacy regulator if you are unhappy with the answer.

We answer within 30 days, or sooner where your law requires it, and we never charge for a first request or treat you differently for making one.

One honest limitation. Because there are no accounts, the data described in section 4 is pseudonymous, and we normally cannot connect it to you as a person; we will not collect extra identifying data just to search for you. That is a privacy benefit, but it means “show me my data” usually has no answer beyond this policy. If you can tell us your Firebase app instance identifier, we can look it up in our copy of the statistics and delete those records. What always works: uninstalling the Game, or clearing its storage on Android, erases everything on your device, withdrawing consent stops future collection, and resetting the advertising identifier in your device settings breaks the link between past ad data and your device.

We have not appointed an Article 27 representative in the Union or the UK: as a one-person developer whose processing is occasional, pseudonymous and low-risk, we rely on the exemption in Art. 27(2) GDPR, and we answer requests directly at the address in section 1.

Automated decisions and pricing. The prices and special offers you see in the shop depend on your own purchase history in the Game and on how recently you have played it: a discount switches on when you come back after a week or more away, or when two weeks have passed since your last purchase, and lasts until your next purchase. We also run experiments comparing price levels, so two players may be shown different prices for the same pack. That is automated, and we tell you plainly because EU consumer law requires it. It is not a decision producing legal or similarly significant effects within the meaning of Art. 22 GDPR: nothing is denied to you, the Game is playable without buying anything, and the exact price is always shown by the store before you confirm. We do not profile you for any other purpose.

12. Children

Arrows In! is a general-audience puzzle game. It is not directed to children, it is not listed in any store’s children’s category, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from a child under 13, or under the age of consent in your country, which is up to 16 in parts of the EEA and 18 under some other laws. We do not ask your age, because asking would mean collecting more data than we need; instead we keep the Game free of anything that targets children. If you are a parent or guardian and believe a child has provided personal data through the Game, write to us and we will delete what we can, and tell you honestly what is beyond our reach.

13. Security

The save record sits in the app’s private storage, which the operating system protects from other apps, and its cloud copy sits in a hidden app folder of your Google account that other apps cannot open. Everything the Game sends to the services above travels over encrypted connections. No method is perfect, but the risk here is limited by design: we hold no accounts, no passwords and no payment data. If a breach ever affects you, we will notify you and the relevant authority as the applicable law requires.
